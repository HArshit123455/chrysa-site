Chrysa compared with other screen time apps

Opal, one sec, Forest and Android's own Digital Wellbeing all solve a real problem, and for some people they solve it better than Chrysa does. Here is the honest version, written by the person who made Chrysa.

The short version

Almost every app in this category works by subtraction: it takes the phone away, for a while, in some form. Chrysa is the only one here that works by addition — it never takes anything away, and instead offers one specific small thing to do with the time.

That makes it a genuinely different tool, not a better one. If what you need is to be stopped, Chrysa will not stop you, and you should use something on this page that will.

When each one is the better choice

Choose Opal if you want to actually be stopped

Opal is the strongest blocker of the group. It schedules sessions, covers websites as well as apps, and has modes that make it genuinely hard to bypass yourself — which is exactly the right design if you have a deadline and know you will try to cheat. Chrysa cannot do this and never will; refusing to block is the premise of the whole app. If you have already decided the answer is "make it impossible", Opal is a better tool than mine.

Choose one sec if you want the smallest possible intervention

one sec does one thing extremely well: it puts a mandatory breath between the reach and the app, then lets you continue. It is the closest app here to Chrysa's posture, it is simpler than Chrysa, and it is on iPhone, which Chrysa is not. The difference is what happens after the pause. one sec hands the moment back to you empty; Chrysa puts something in it.

Choose Forest if gamification works on you

Forest is for deliberate focus sessions rather than for catching an autopilot reach, and for a lot of people the growing tree is genuinely motivating. It is honest about being a game. Chrysa deliberately has none of this — no tree dies, nothing decays, and nothing you built goes away if you stop using it for three weeks — because for some people a dying tree is one more thing to feel bad about. Which of those two describes you is not something I can guess.

Choose Digital Wellbeing if you want nothing extra installed

It is free, already on your phone, costs no battery, and asks for no permissions beyond what Android already has. If timers are enough for you, that is the correct answer and you do not need any of us. Its limitation is that it is purely a measuring and limiting tool: it will tell you that you spent four hours on Instagram and it will cut you off at three, and neither of those is an idea about what to do on a Sunday afternoon.

Where Chrysa is the better choice

In one case, specifically: you have already tried blockers and taken them off. If timers and blocks and dying trees have each lasted you about a week, the thing that keeps failing is probably not your discipline — it is that none of them had an answer for the ten minutes after. That is the gap Chrysa is built for.

It is also the only one here that is free with nothing to unlock, has no account at all, and keeps every number on the phone with no server involved.

Where Chrysa is worse, plainly

  • It cannot stop you. If you want to be stopped, this is the wrong app, and no setting will change that.
  • Android only. There is no iPhone version.
  • The phone-time numbers need a permission — Android's usage access — which some people reasonably decline. Everything else in the app works without it.
  • It is new, and made by one person. Version 1.0 went live in October 2026. There are not many ratings yet, and some things are still rough.

Common questions

What is a free alternative to Opal on Android?

Chrysa is free on Android with nothing to unlock, but it works differently: it never blocks apps, and instead offers something else to do when you open one on autopilot. Android's built-in Digital Wellbeing is the free option that actually blocks, using daily app timers.

Is there a screen time app that does not block apps?

Chrysa never blocks anything. It offers a small alternative sized to the time you have, and you can always open the app anyway. one sec is the nearest comparison — it adds a pause before the app, then lets you through.

Is there a screen time app with no streaks?

Chrysa has no streaks, no score and nothing that decays if you stop using it. Declining an offer says "Not today" and costs nothing.

Is there a screen time app that does not need an account?

Chrysa has no account and no server. Everything lives in a database on your phone, and the screen time, sleep and step numbers never leave the device.
