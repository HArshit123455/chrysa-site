Chrysa

Something better to do than scrolling.

Chrysa is a screen time app for Android that never blocks anything. Open an app on autopilot and it offers one small thing, sized to the time you actually have.

Your home screen is a window.

The pause

Choose the apps you open without deciding to. The moment one opens, Chrysa offers a two-minute alternative. You can open the app anyway — nothing is ever blocked, and “Not today” is a complete answer.

The day

Sleep, steps and screen time, read from the phone so you never type them. Plus the open blocks left in your afternoon, and an idea for what would make one feel worthwhile.

The view

The sky above is whatever hour you are really in — the one at the top of this page follows your clock the same way. Outside it, the land fills with what you actually did: a tree for a walk, a lit window for an evening with someone, a bird for twenty pages.

The words

“Walked 20 minutes with a friend at 7” is enough. Or fill in the whole day in five minutes at night.

What it refuses to do.

  • No score. Five areas of life, never summed into one number.
  • No streaks. Nothing to keep up, and nothing out there decays.
  • No blocking. A moment between impulse and action, then out of the way.
  • No subscription, no ads, nothing behind a paywall.
  • No account, and no server of ours for your days to sit on.

The best outcome we can imagine is that you open Chrysa less, because your life got better.

Privacy, plainly.

Everything lives in a database on your phone. There is no account and no backend. Screen time, sleep and steps are read on the device and never leave it.

There is exactly one exception, and it is a switch you control, off by default: nearby places and weather. With it on, Chrysa sends a location rounded to about a kilometre to Open-Meteo, and to about 110 metres to OpenStreetMap — a block, because a kilometre of error is the difference between your neighbourhood and your city. Nothing else ever leaves the phone.

You can export everything as a file, or delete all of it, any time.

Questions.

Does Chrysa block apps?

No. Chrysa never blocks anything. When you open an app you tend to open on autopilot, it offers one small alternative sized to the time you have, and you can open the app anyway.

Is Chrysa free?

Yes. Chrysa is free on Google Play, with no ads, no subscription and nothing to unlock.

Does Chrysa need an account?

No. There is no account and no server. Everything lives in a database on your phone.

Does Chrysa work offline?

Yes, entirely. The one exception is an off-by-default switch for nearby places and weather, which sends only a rounded location.

What does Chrysa track?

Sleep, steps and screen time read from the phone, plus anything you log in plain words. It shows five areas of life as dots — never one overall score, and no streaks.

Is Chrysa a digital wellbeing app?

Loosely, yes, though it works the other way round from most of them. Instead of limiting what you do on the phone, it offers something to do away from it. Nothing is restricted and nothing is scored.

Does Chrysa help with doomscrolling?

That is what it is for. When you open an app you reach for out of habit, Chrysa offers one small alternative sized to the time you have. You can carry on anyway; the point is that for a moment there was something else on offer.

Is Chrysa on iPhone?

Android only for now.
